GitHub issues are open to everyone, so you can end up giving free, time-consuming support to people who have never sponsored or contributed to your project. GitHub has no built-in way to limit who opens a support request, so you either answer everything or moderate issues by hand.

Sponsor Gated Support is my answer: a GitHub Action that keeps labeled support issues for the people who back your work.

{/* truncate */}

## Why I Built It

Support takes time, and sponsorship offered my supporters nothing tangible in return. I wanted support requests reserved for the people funding the work, without closing and locking issues myself.

The usual workarounds fall short. Static allowlists go stale, turning issues off shuts out bug reports too, and a sponsorship paywall ignores people who contribute in non-monetary ways.

## What It Does

When an issue with a gated label is opened, the action checks its author against your live GitHub Sponsors list, your contributors, and an exempt file. Out of the box, it:

- **Welcomes the people who back your work** — Sponsors get your welcome message, and their issue stays open.
- **Recognizes contributors** — Members, owners, contributors, and collaborators bypass the sponsorship requirement, and the exempt file grants access to anyone else you choose.
- **Closes and locks the rest** — Everyone else gets your not-sponsor message, and the issue is closed and locked.
- **Gates only the labels you choose** — Only issues with a label from `ISSUE_LABELS` (default: `support`) are gated, so bug reports and feature requests stay open to everyone.
- **Keeps gated threads on topic** — Comments from anyone other than the opener, members, owners, contributors, or collaborators are deleted, and gated issues lock when they close.

Both messages are yours to write, and you can require an active sponsorship and a minimum monthly tier amount. Organization-owned repositories work too, through the `IS_ORGANIZATION` mode.

## Two Tokens, One Workflow

A classic personal access token with the `read:org` and `read:user` scopes reads your sponsors. The workflow's built-in `GITHUB_TOKEN` moderates as the github-actions bot, not as you.

Store the personal access token as a repository secret, then add a workflow that runs on issue and comment events:

```yaml title=".github/workflows/sponsor-gated-support.yml"
name: "Sponsor Gated Support"

on:
  issue_comment:
    types:
      - "created"
      - "edited"
  issues:
    types:
      - "opened"
      - "closed"

permissions:
  issues: "write"

jobs:
  check-issues:
    runs-on: "ubuntu-latest"
    steps:
      - name: "Checkout repository"
        uses: "actions/checkout@v4"
      - name: "Check support issues"
        uses: "mrjackyliang/sponsor-gated-support@v2"
        with:
          GITHUB_PERSONAL_ACCESS_TOKEN: "${{ secrets.PERSONAL_ACCESS_TOKEN }}"
          GITHUB_WORKFLOW_TOKEN: "${{ secrets.GITHUB_TOKEN }}"
```

The checkout step lets the action read your `SPONSOR_EXEMPT` file. The other nine inputs are optional, and the [Inputs](/docs/reference/inputs/) reference lists each default.

## Where It Stands Today

I first released Sponsor Gated Support in August 2024, and it's now at version 2.0.10 on the Node 24 Actions runtime. Workflows reference it by the `v2` tag.

Head to [Usage](/docs/getting-started/usage/) to add it to your repository. If you still need the token, start with the [Personal Access Token](/docs/reference/personal-access-token/) guide.
